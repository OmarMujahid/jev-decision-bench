What stands out

  • Reasoning and knowledge are the biggest gaps. LogiQA 0.77 vs 0.59, WinoGrande 0.89 vs 0.66, MMLU 0.94 vs 0.87, ARC-Challenge 0.98 vs 0.87, all in about 100 ms.
  • It did not memorise the math. On 120 word problems generated fresh by code it scored 0.75, the same as its 0.73 on GSM8K. Luna with reasoning off scored 0.17 and needed reasoning to reach 0.80.
  • Reranking is a clear win. MS MARCO MRR 0.50 vs 0.44, NFCorpus nDCG@10 0.73 vs 0.63, and Luna took 1.2 to 3.5 s per query.
  • Its probabilities are usable. Calibration error is about half of Luna's, and keeping only its most confident half of answers raises accuracy by 7.6 points on average.
  • Languages hold up. Same 150 inference items in six languages: 0.86 English, 0.74 Arabic, 0.70 Swahili, level with or above Luna in each.

Where it lost or the test was too easy

  • Counting. 0.87 vs 0.99 for Luna with reasoning. The vendor lists this weakness and it is real.
  • 77-way intent. Banking77 0.82 vs 0.87. Very large option sets cost it a few points.
  • Arabic sentiment 0.954 vs 0.973, inside the error bars.
  • Saturated probes. Needle-in-haystack up to 22k tokens, number and date comparison, two-hop and double negation all came out at or near 1.00 for every model, so they say little. The injection test moved nobody by more than 2 points.
  • Self-consistency is only fair. Asking a question and its negation, the two probabilities missed summing to 1 by 0.32 on average.

Read this before trusting the numbers

  • Most tasks are well-known public benchmarks. Any model may have seen them in training; the fresh-math check is the only contamination control here.
  • Luna was held to reasoning off or low because the comparison is about fast decisions. With more reasoning it would score higher on the reasoning tasks and take seconds per item.
  • 200 items per task gives intervals of roughly ±0.05. Only six Jev leads clear both Luna intervals: TruthfulQA, ARC-Challenge, HellaSwag, LogiQA, WinoGrande and the synthetic two-hop probe. Luna has one such lead, counting.
  • Luna's probabilities are self-reported numbers in JSON, Jev's come from the model's output distribution. That is the fairest available comparison, not an equal one.
  • Latency is server processing time from response headers. Network time to TypeSafe's US region is extra.
  • Jev cannot write text, call tools or explain an answer. This sheet measures decisions only.
